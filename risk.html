5. Risk Management

Scoring approach

Each risk is scored from 1 to 5 for probability and from 1 to 5 for impact, and the two are multiplied to give an exposure score out of 25. Impact is judged against the two hardest constraints on this project: the 25 September deadline and the $106,986 budget ceiling. A risk scoring 15 or above needs a funded response in place before the affected work package starts. A risk scoring 9 to 14 needs a named owner and a documented response. Anything below 9 is monitored at the weekly review.

What the register shows

No risk reached the critical band, but the register is weighted by impact rather than probability. R1 and R4 both carry an impact of 5, because either a prolonged outage or a corrupted data set would stop a live store trading. Both were therefore answered with something physical and funded – backup routers in one case, a retained rollback snapshot in the other – rather than with monitoring alone. Neither response was optional or left until the risk appeared.

R2 is the highest-probability risk on the register, and it comes from a group with no authority over the project: the pilot store cashiers, classified low power and high interest on the stakeholder map. Risk and stakeholder analysis point at the same people, which is why the response is rehearsal and floor support rather than an email, and why the store bulletin steps up to daily contact during the first week after go-live.

The link to money runs the other way. R1's response was funded in the network category at planning time, then topped up from the contingency through change request CR-02 during execution, when latency appeared at two stores. The risk register is therefore not a static document: it is what made that mid-project purchase a planned response instead of an emergency.
