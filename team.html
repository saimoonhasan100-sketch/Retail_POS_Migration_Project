R = responsible (does the work), A = accountable (owns the outcome, one per phase), C = consulted before the decision, I = informed after it.

Accountability never splits: the Project Manager is the single A on every phase, while responsibility moves with the technical work. Phase 3 is the only phase with three responsible parties, which matches it being the busiest stretch of the critical path and the phase where UAT deliberately puts a second pair of eyes (Operations) on the specialist's work.

The allocation table also exposes a structural weakness. The Cloud Specialist holds 7 of the 21 assignments and 128 of the 368 scheduled hours. That was workable across five stores with one cutover, but it would not scale to a national rollout, so cross-training the cloud and hardware roles is carried into the closing recommendations.

The frequencies are set by the power-interest analysis rather than by preference. Manage-closely stakeholders get daily contact. Keep-satisfied stakeholders get a bi-weekly summary, because they need assurance on budget and milestones, not detail. Keep-informed store staff get a weekly bulletin that steps up to daily floor support at go-live, which is the practical answer to risk R2.

Work performance information

  • Trello board – card movement by list gives work in progress and completion counts per phase.
  • ProjectLibre – task completion against the schedule, dependency slippage and resource work hours.
  • Excel workbook – task status register, budget drawdown by category, and the variance figures reported each week.
